# Lesson 3: Directing the script (approval 1)

**Goal:** approve a script that strangers will watch to the end and vote on. This is the cheapest moment to change the
story: nothing has been voiced or drawn yet.

## What Claude shows you

A table of beats (the time, what we see, what's said) and the vote at the end. Here is part of Day 23's first episode
(the whole script is in the skill folder, in `examples/day-23/ep01/BEATS.md`, or just ask Claude to "show me the Day 23
episode 1 script"):

| Time | What we see | What's said |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 s | The door handle rattles. A shape behind the glass. | "You wake up. Something is at the door." |
| 3.2 s | The calendar: days 1-22 crossed out, 23 circled. | "Twenty-three days. That's how long you slept." |
| ... | ... | ... |
| 24.0 s | One of the dead turns. Then the whole street. | "One turns. Then all of them." |
| 27.3 s | You and the dog. The vote: 1 THE POLICE CAR, 2 THE BUS, 3 BACK INSIDE. | "Run for the police car. Hide in the bus. Or go back inside." |

(At approval 1 the times are rough. They're fixed when the voice is recorded in lesson 4.)

Read it twice: once fast, like a stranger scrolling, and once slowly, as the director.

## The first 4 seconds decide everything

Most people decide in the first two seconds whether to keep watching. Check:

- **Frame 0 is already happening.** A threat or a problem is in the picture with sound: no title, no scenery, no
  "previously on".
- **A stranger gets the premise by about 2 seconds.** The first episode of Day 23 started on action, but nothing on
  screen said what the series was (the end of the world, a dog, a vote) until about 20 seconds in. We think that's why
  strangers swiped away. Put the premise in the first line and the first picture, for example "The world ended. You
  slept through it." (This version has no ready-made premise card. The built-in story card fits about 22 characters
  and then shrinks to a small tag. A custom card is extra work outside the skill's steps.)
- **Episode 2 onwards: credit the vote by about 4 seconds.** A designed "YOUR VOTE" card over moving action ("Most of you
  said: follow the prints"). The viewers who voted see their choice count.
- **Keep "PART 2" out of the first second.** People swipe away from sequels they haven't seen. The opening sells the
  premise; the part number lives on the cover and in the caption.

## The middle: something every 3 seconds

- **A new event about every 3 seconds, and a real turn about every 7.** If a stretch is people talking about what
  happened, that's the stretch to cut.
- **Every line is something you could draw.** "A boot print, fresh" works. "You feel uneasy about the future" doesn't.
- **One moment worth clipping**, about 60% of the way through: 3-4 seconds, big and full-screen, then 1.5-2 seconds
  with no voice.
- **Fair surprises.** Every twist pays off something the viewer already saw. The hero gets out through a choice, never
  through luck.

## The words

- **"You", present tense, the whole way** ("Your arm goes numb. It slips.").
- **Short sentences, not a pile of fragments.** Claude aims for 4-5 words a sentence on average and uses fragments for
  rhythm ("Three percent. Four. Nine."). If the whole script reads as clipped fragments, ask for full sentences. On one
  of our series the note was "the narration feels very small"; full sentences fixed it, even though the episode ran a
  little longer.
- **About 3 words a second at most.** If a line is too long for its moment, cut words. Don't ask for faster speech.
- **The narrator never explains the mystery.** It states what happens and moves on; the comments argue about the rest.

## The vote

- **Two options, three at most.** Each is a physical action about what's in the last frame: "run for the police car",
  "hide in the bus", "go back inside".
- **Each option pulls a different way**: learning something versus protecting someone, for example. If everyone would
  pick the same one, it's not a real choice.
- **Taught before it's asked.** Every option must already make sense from this episode.
- **Said three ways:** spoken, as a big title, and as numbered pills that pop up as each option is spoken.

A near-even split (55/45) means the vote was good. 90/10 means one option was a trap.

## How to give notes

Be specific: point at a line by its time, or quote its first words. For example:
- "The line at 3.2 s explains too much. Show the calendar and say less."
- "The first 2 seconds don't say what the series is. Put the premise in the first line."
- "Options 1 and 3 are basically the same. Make one of them about protecting the dog."
- "The line at 24 s is a pile of fragments. Make it one full sentence."

Then say **"OK"** when you're happy. That's approval 1.

**Once the voice is locked (lesson 4), the words are fixed.** The pictures are built on that timing, so a story change
after that means new takes and re-timed shots. Make story changes now, while they cost nothing.
