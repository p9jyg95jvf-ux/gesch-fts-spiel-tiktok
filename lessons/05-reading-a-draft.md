# Lesson 5: Reading a draft and giving notes (approval 3)

**Goal:** catch everything a stranger would notice before the final is rendered, and say it in a way that gets it
fixed on the first try.

## What Claude already checked

Before you see a draft, Claude has:
- rendered it and checked every frame is there and the sound is at the right loudness;
- flagged any frame that's too dark;
- checked the safe frame: no words or faces under the app's buttons or at the edges;
- looked at sheets of every single frame for doubled images, captions on faces, missing props and broken poses.

So you don't need to check the numbers. Your job is the one thing a check can't do: **watch it like a stranger.**

## How to watch a draft

1. **On your phone, with sound, full screen.** That's how everyone else will see it. Claude tells you where the draft
   is: a small file named like `my-series_ep01_draft.mp4` in your episode's `out` folder. Send it to your phone the
   same way as the voice in lesson 4.
2. **Once straight through, without stopping.** Then ask yourself: Did I get what happened? Did I want to know what
   comes next? Was there a moment I'd send to a friend? Where did my attention drop?
3. **Once more, pausing.** Now look for things that are wrong in the picture. The draft is a quick half-size render
   (540x960), so it looks a little soft, and very fast moves can look slightly doubled. That's normal: the final is
   full size (1080x1920) with smoother motion blur. Note softness or doubling only if you still see it in the final.
4. Write your notes as you go, with the time from the player.

## What to look for

These are real notes from the first Day 23 episodes. Every one was fixed before posting. All but one were caught at
the draft; the missing bandana was spotted only after the first final, which is why you watch the final too (see the
end of this lesson).

| The note | What was wrong | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| "The cut at 30-33 s is bad, people vanish." | The shot cut away in the middle of the vote, so you and the dog disappeared from the street. | Never cut away in the middle of an action: the vote and the end card became one continuous shot. |
| "The man and the wheelchair disappear at 9.4 s." | Same problem in the hallway: a cut, and the hero was gone. | One continuous shot that follows you down the hall. Then every other cut was checked for the same thing. |
| "The dog still has 6 legs." | Round joints at the ends of the legs read as extra paws, and the tail hung down like another leg. | The legs were redrawn as one shape each, with the far pair darker, checked from every angle. |
| "Make the feet better, remove the two circles." | Each leg ended in a round cap that stuck out below the shoe. | One foot shape with a flat sole, for people and the dog. |
| Floating cars, people walking through cars | Cars drawn flat, not standing on the road; people and cars drawn in the wrong order. | Cars sit on the road with a shadow; people and cars are drawn in one front-to-back order. |
| "Add some music." | The episode had sound effects but no music. | An original score made in code, quiet under the voice and full where nobody speaks. |
| The dog's red bandana was missing | A fix to the legs had hidden it under the chin. | Moved back. It's the dog's signature colour, so it shows in every shot. |
| A "scared" face looked like a grin | The expression read wrong at phone size. | "Worried" instead, and quicker face changes. |
| "We need better sound effects." | The episode needed real recorded sounds in a few places. | Free Pixabay sounds, fetched with the creator's OK and logged, placed on the moments in the picture. |

The pattern: **most good notes are "something looks wrong"**, a person vanishing, a limb too many, something floating,
something missing. You don't need to know why it happened. Say what you saw and when.

Also watch for:
- a caption covering a face or the thing you're meant to look at;
- a caption still showing after its line is over;
- someone walking backwards (everyone should face the way they move);
- hands missing, or an object floating instead of being held;
- a moment that's too dark to read;
- a sound that lands early or late (a bark when the mouth opens, a thud when the hand hits the door).

## How to write a note

**Time + what you see + what you expected.**
- Good: "At 0:31 you and the dog vanish when it cuts to the crowd. I expected to still see you."
- Not as good: "The ending feels off."

More tips:
- **One problem per note.** Number them, so Claude can answer each one.
- **Ask for the pattern to be checked**, not just the spot: "Check every other cut for the same thing."
- **Say what's working too.** "The music in the street is perfect, keep it." That stops a good part from changing.
- **Story changes cost more now.** The voice is locked. A change to what's said means new takes and re-timed shots.
  Picture fixes, sound fixes and caption fixes are cheap.

Claude fixes the notes, re-checks, and sends the next draft. Day 23's first episode took six rounds of notes; that's
normal for a first episode, and later episodes need fewer as the kit (characters, places, props) is reused.

When you'd post it as it is, say **"OK, render the final"**. That's approval 3.

**One rule before posting:** watch the final end to end with sound. Never post something you haven't watched.
