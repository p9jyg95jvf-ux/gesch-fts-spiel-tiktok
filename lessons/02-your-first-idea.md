# Lesson 2: Your first idea, the bible and the rule card

**Goal:** turn one or two sentences into a series bible you're happy with. The bible is the one file every episode
is built from.

## 1. Say your idea

Open Claude Code in the empty folder you made in lesson 1 and give the idea in one or two sentences:

> Make me a series: a kid and their robot run a night-shift repair shop, and viewers vote each episode.

A good idea already has four things:
- **Who "you" are.** The viewer is the hero, told as "you".
- **Where you are**, somewhere you could draw: a hospital, a lighthouse, a repair shop.
- **What's at stake right now:** a threat, a deadline, a secret.
- **A companion**, a dog, a robot or a little brother: someone whose face shows what the moment feels like.

Claude may ask up to three quick questions, but only when the answer changes everything: who you are in the story, the
tone, and anything you must or mustn't have. Otherwise it decides and tells you what it decided.

Claude makes your series folder inside it, named after the series (for example `night-shift-repair-shop`). Every file
in these lessons, like `series/BIBLE.md`, is in that folder. If you haven't added your ElevenLabs key yet, do it now
(lesson 1, step 3): the voices in lesson 4 need it.

**Original only.** If your idea leans on a film, game or show you love, Claude offers an original take instead. That's
on purpose: a world you made up is a world you own.

## 2. What the bible holds

Claude writes `series/BIBLE.md`. It's the source of truth, and it's **secret: never post it**. Its parts:

| Part | What it is | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| The promise | the premise in one line, "you" as the hero, the vote loop, the feel in 2-3 words | Would a stranger get it in one breath? |
| The laws | 3-6 things that are always true in this world | Is each one simple, visual, and going to matter? |
| The cast | who's who: role, look, voice, what they want, what they never do | Does the companion have a clear face and colour? |
| The mystery spine | the big secret, and one answer plus one new question per episode | Do you want to know the answer yourself? |
| The clue plan | what gets planted early and paid off later | Will each payoff feel fair? |
| Vote rules, nevers, look and sound | the house rules for every episode | Is anything there you hate? Say so now. |

Claude shows you a 5-8 line summary. Read it like a viewer, not like an editor. Change anything now: the bible is cheap
to change, but a posted episode isn't.

## 3. The rule card

Some laws of your world get a **RULE card**: a big card that slams onto the middle of the screen, then shrinks to a
small tag in the top-left corner (under the day, time and place panel) until the next card or the vote. Day 23 (the
example series that comes with the skill, in `examples/day-23/`) has this one in episode 1:

> **RULE #1 · STAY UNSEEN**
> One of the dead seeing you is enough: when one turns, they all turn.

A rule card works when it is:
- **Short.** About 22 characters or fewer: "RULE #1 · STAY UNSEEN" (21) fits. Longer, and the words shrink and crowd
  the edges of the card, or run off it. A rule that needs a full sentence on the card is too long: put the sentence in
  the narration instead.
- **Surprising and simple.** Something the viewer didn't expect, which they can repeat to a friend.
- **Used in action, in the same episode.** In Day 23 the card lands, and seconds later the whole street turns. A rule
  that's only stated is a fact, not a rule.
- **Planted one beat earlier.** The viewer sees a hint before the card names it.

One rule card per episode is plenty. Some series use none, and that's fine.

## 4. The facts file

Claude also makes `series/CANON.md`. It starts empty and fills up after each episode is posted: what the audience has
actually seen and heard (names, rules, who knows what, which day it is).

Viewers notice every slip. If a rule was shown one way in episode 2, it can't quietly change in episode 5. Each new
script has to agree with CANON.md; if you spot a clash, say so at approval 1. If the story needs a change, the change
happens on screen, with a reason the viewer can see.

## Notes that work at this stage

- "Make the companion braver: it should make its own choices."
- "The tone is too jokey. I want quiet and tense."
- "Law 3 is too complicated. Make it something you can see."
- "I don't want any blood. Scary by numbers and by silence."
