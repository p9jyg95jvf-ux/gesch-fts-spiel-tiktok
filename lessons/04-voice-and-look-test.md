# Lesson 4: Voice and look test (approval 2)

**Goal:** the voices sound right, the timing is locked, and you've seen every character and every place at the size
they'll really appear, before a single shot is built.

## Part 1: the voice

**Casting (once per series).** Claude lists the voices on your ElevenLabs account and suggests a narrator (calm, with
weight, clear) and one voice for each character who speaks. The same voices are used every episode, so pick ones you'll
still like by episode 10. To hear a voice, find it on the Voices page of your ElevenLabs account (My Voices) and press
play. If you like one from the public voice library, add it to your voices first, then ask Claude to list the voices
again.

**Before anything is spent, Claude tells you the cost** in ElevenLabs characters, for example "about 520 characters".
Nothing is recorded until you say yes. Takes are kept on your computer, so running the voice step again only pays for
new takes. Changing a line's words also re-records the lines just before and after it in the same voice, because their
delivery depends on it. Claude shows you the new cost every time. The word check after recording (below) also uses a
little of your ElevenLabs speech-to-text allowance each time it runs. It's measured in minutes of audio, not
characters, so it isn't in that number.

**Recording.** One take per line. Then a speech-to-text check compares every word with the script and should report
"no differences". If a word is wrong or a line stumbles, Claude re-takes only that line.

**Listen to it.** Ask Claude where `vo.wav` is (it's in your episode folder, for example `ep01/vo.wav`). Send it to
yourself the way you'd send any file (AirDrop from a Mac to an iPhone, or email, WhatsApp, Google Drive, OneDrive or
iCloud Drive) and play it on your phone, ideally with earphones. Listen for:
- a word said wrong, or a name pronounced two different ways;
- a line that sounds rushed, or one that sounds bored;
- the big line of the episode: does it land?

**A new take isn't always a better take.** Your comment line (on Day 23: "Comment one or two.") comes back every
episode: keep its words the same whenever the episode has the same number of options, so viewers know it when they hear
it. If you liked an earlier take of a line in this episode better, say so: Claude can switch back to it for free, as
long as the lines around it haven't changed. (Picking a take from an earlier episode isn't built in yet: each episode
records its own.)

**The lock.** When the voice is right, Claude locks the timing. From here every picture is built to the voice, so the
words don't change any more. A small re-take later still fits (it stays on its locked start); a rewrite means new
takes and re-timed shots.

## Part 2: the look test

Claude draws what the episode needs: the characters (model sheets showing every angle, pose and expression, plus a
line-up of the whole cast) and one still of each place with the cast in it, in that scene's light. You get one board
image with all of it.

**This is the moment to be picky.** A note on the board costs minutes; the same note on a finished draft costs a
re-render. On Day 23 two notes looked small and weren't: "the car interior looks very bad" and "the guy looks very
small". Both would have meant rebuilding shots had they come at the draft.

So open the board on your phone the same way, and zoom in until one still fills the screen: that's the size viewers
will see. Check:

- **Is every new place on the board?** If the episode goes somewhere new, a kitchen, a roof, a boat, ask to see it.
- **Is the hero shown at the size they'll really be?** A character who reads fine on a big sheet can be a speck in a
  wide shot.
- **Faces read at phone size.** You can tell what each face feels.
- **Nothing is too dark.** Every dark scene shows where its light comes from: a lamp, a screen, a window.
- **The hero and companion are the brightest things** in each still, so the eye finds them first.
- **The places have detail you can read:** shelves, signs, windows, things on the floor. Plain blocks of colour look
  unfinished.
- **Hands are there**, attached to arms, holding things properly.
- **Expressions read the way they should.** On Day 23 a "scared" face read as a grin; "worried" or "surprised" fixed
  it.
- **The clue objects are readable.** If the story needs the viewer to notice a key, the key must read on a phone.

## How to give notes

Point at the image and say what you see:
- "The dog's face is too small in still 3. Bring the camera closer."
- "The workshop is too dark. Add a window."
- "The robot's colour is too close to the wall. Make it orange."
- "Show me the rooftop too. It's in the episode and not on the board."

Claude fixes them and sends a new board. When it's right, say **"OK"**. That's approval 2.
